# Method

One page. The working protocol is longer and stays private; this is what a
reader needs to judge whether the calls here are worth anything.

## Coverage

Three companies, covered properly rather than fifteen covered thinly: **Kymera
(KYMR)**, **Monte Rosa (GLUE)** and **C4 Therapeutics (CCCC)** — immunology,
cardiovascular inflammation and oncology. Competitor catalysts are tracked
because the sector trades as a block, but they do not get notes.

## Every note makes a call

A note that does not reach a directional view is not research. The science is
the input; the output is a claim about value.

Each note separates **three independent claims** and names which is weakest:

1. **Scientific** — the biology will work
2. **Catalyst** — the market recognises it, in a defined window
3. **Valuation** — it is not already in the price

Most scientific analysis nails the first and ignores the other two.

## Five gates

A note publishes only if it clears all five:

| Gate | Test |
|---|---|
| Falsifiability | A date and an observation that would prove it wrong |
| Variance | Consensus stated **as a number**, and the view differs from it |
| Edge | The source of edge named — informational, analytical or behavioural |
| Persistence | Why the mispricing survives, and who is on the other side |
| Asymmetry | The payoff is skewed, and the skew is quantified |

Every draft is reviewed adversarially before publication by a reader who sees
the draft and the public record only, not the reasoning that produced it.

## Two classes of prediction, never pooled

**Thesis** calls are argued in a note. They are the track record.

**Calendar** calls are standing forecasts on dated events — whether a guided
readout lands in its quarter, whether a trial starts when the company said. Real
and scored, but not research.

They are reported side by side and never averaged together. Twenty near-certain
calendar calls score around 0.05 between them; six genuine thesis calls score
around 0.22. Pooled, the headline reads 0.09 and describes neither. The one
statistic that does pool fairly is the calibration curve — whether a stated 70%
means 70% is a fair question at any level of difficulty.

Calendar calls are only logged when the stated probability falls between 20% and
85%. Outside that band the call is near-free and teaches nothing.

## Scoring

Brier score, reported by class, plus a calibration curve by confidence band. If
something is called at 70% twenty times, it should happen fourteen times.

Each resolved prediction is also marked for whether the **reasoning** was sound,
independently of whether the outcome was right. Right for the wrong reason is
recorded as a miss on process. On the sample sizes available here, process is
the more informative measure.

## Conventions

- Enterprise value, never market capitalisation
- Net price, never list price
- Cash and runway always with an as-of date
- Every date carries a source and a separate confidence level
- Scenario probabilities sum to 100% and are written before the event
- No material non-public information, ever

## Authorship

Notes are drafted with AI and reviewed, edited and published by the author, who
is accountable for every claim. He can defend any sentence in any note here.
