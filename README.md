# Targeted protein degradation — equity research

Dated, falsifiable calls on targeted protein degradation companies, published
before the events they describe and scored afterwards, whether they were right
or wrong.

Written by a PhD student working in a targeted protein degradation lab. The
edge, such as it is, is being able to read a degradation paper's methods section
and judge whether the assay is any good.

## Start here

**[SCORECARD.md](SCORECARD.md)** — every prediction, its date, its stated
probability, and how it resolved. This is the point of the repository; the notes
are the argument behind it.

**[notes/](notes/)** — the published notes, newest first. Each one makes a
directional call, states what the market expects as a number, and names the
observation that would prove it wrong.

**[METHOD.md](METHOD.md)** — one page on how this is done and scored.

## The rules that make this worth reading

- **Predictions publish before the event.** The publication date on the
  companion post is the timestamp of record.
- **Nothing is deleted.** Predictions retired before their event are marked
  withdrawn and stay visible with the reason. Misses stay up.
- **Notes are never edited after publication.** Corrections are new notes that
  link to the original.
- **Thesis calls and calendar calls are scored separately** and never pooled
  into one number. Pooling easy calls with hard ones flatters the average and
  describes neither.
- **Outcome and reasoning are scored independently.** Being right for the wrong
  reason is recorded as a failure of process.

## Method

Notes are drafted with AI and reviewed, edited and published by the author, who
is accountable for every claim in them. Every figure carries a source, and
figures that are the author's own estimates rather than looked-up values say so
in those words.
